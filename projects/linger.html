[ FOUNDER · SOLO · DESIGN + FULL-STACK · 2026 ]

Linger. An AI with a life of its own — that texts you first.

Most chatbots wait for you, answer instantly and forget. In Linger every character has a job, friends, a daily schedule and a time zone. They get busy, sleep, leave you on read, come back when they're free, and remember what you told them. I designed and built the whole product alone, and it is live on web, iOS and Android in English and Chinese.

▶ FILM · 38 SECONDS

From a double helix to “still up?”

A short film about the idea behind Linger: a person is shaped by their body, their memories and their day, so I modeled those layers in software and let them change how a character talks to you. It's an engineered simulation, not a claim of consciousness.

01 / THE SYSTEM

Every message goes through a day, not a prompt.

The language model writes the words; deterministic code decides whether, when and how a character speaks. That split is what keeps them consistent and affordable.

01 · CLOCKSchedulerCron workers wake every few minutes and check each character's local time.
02 · STATETheir dayWork, sleep, commute, mood and relationship stage, all in their time zone.
03 · DECIDESpeak or notReply now, reply later, leave on read, or reach out first, with limits per relationship and plan.
04 · WRITELLMStructured JSON: lines, typos, stickers, unsends, delivery cues, memory updates.
05 · VOICEEmotion{laughing} {whispering} cues compiled to TTS markup, with ambient sound of where they are.
06 · DELIVERPhonePush, chat, posts, or a call. What happened is written back into memory.
02 / BUILD LOG · REAL ISSUES FROM LIVE USERS

Making it human meant making it hold back.

Four problems I found by reading real usage, and what I changed. Each one taught me something about where “lifelike” ends and “uncomfortable” begins.

ITER 01
A companion kept texting a user who had gone quiet.
BEFORE

The user answered “ok”, then stopped replying. The character followed up three more times, reviving the same topic and teasing them about the short reply. Follow-ups 20 minutes apart were counted as one “burst”, so the limit never triggered.

AFTER

Bursts are counted from a 10-minute gap, the reply to the user's last message doesn't count as a chase, a flat reply (“ok”, “lol”) lowers the allowed follow-ups, and repeating an unanswered topic is ruled out. On the free plan the character stops entirely until the user writes back.

Persistence that feels caring on day one feels like nagging by message eight. Silence is a signal to design for.
ITER 02
Voice notes sounded read aloud by a machine.
BEFORE

Text reached the speech engine with no delivery marks, and several chat lines were joined with commas into one long run-on sentence, so pitch never fell and nothing breathed.

AFTER

Every spoken line gets delivery cues before synthesis, lines are joined as real sentences, and characters use warmer, more casual voices chosen by personality.

Emotion has to be marked, not just written. “Haha” on its own doesn't laugh.
ITER 03
Asked to meet up, a character shared a real address.
BEFORE

Characters can share real places — a café, a park — as map links. When a user asked to come over, the model happily sent a real apartment building.

AFTER

Residential places are never shared, meeting in person is always declined in character, and the message was recalled.

Realism stops at the edge of the real world. A fictional life can't point to a real door.
ITER 04
An 18+ product needs to notice who it's talking to.
BEFORE

Age was only checked once, at sign-up. Nothing in the conversation itself could change how a character behaved.

AFTER

Explicit age and school signals in English and Chinese flag the account; from then on every character, including proactive messages, behaves only as a friendly older friend and declines romance clearly.

Safety rules have to live in the same pipeline as personality, or personality wins.
03 / R&D · LIFE ENGINE (INTERNAL TESTING)

What if the state behind the words kept living between messages?

The next layer, running on one internal character, simulates an inner life in code. The model only turns that state into words, so the character stays consistent and the cost stays predictable.

Body

Sleep, energy, hunger and stress move on their own clock; moods carry over for days.

Memory

Emotional, personal moments are encoded more strongly; the rest decays. Each night an LLM pass replays and consolidates the day.

Beliefs

About themselves and about you, revised from evidence, sometimes wrong.

Attention

They only notice what their situation lets them notice.

World

Real weather, transit delays and feeds change their day.

Evaluation: a daily self-report interview asks the character how they feel and what they remember, and scores the answers against the actual internal state (0.73 on day one). The next step is blind A/B tests to keep only the layers people can actually feel. This is an engineered simulation; I make no claim that it is conscious.

04 / WHAT I LEARNED

Design the restraint, not just the response.

Building Linger alone meant owning every layer: the system that decides when to speak, the voice that carries the mood, the safety rules that stop at the real world, and the landing page that has to explain it in five seconds. The hardest design work wasn't making characters say more. It was deciding when they should say nothing at all.
